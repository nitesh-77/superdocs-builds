# Photosynthesis — rough draft (scribbled during lunch)

Class 8 science, chapter on photosynthesis. This is everything jumbled together,
needs restructuring before Monday.

So the point of this lesson: students should be able to define photosynthesis,
write out the word equation (carbon dioxide + water -> glucose + oxygen, in
presence of sunlight and chlorophyll), explain why leaves are green, describe
an experiment showing starch in a leaf, and say what happens to the glucose
afterwards (respiration, stored as starch, converted etc.).

Things I need to bring from the lab: potted plant that has been kept in the
dark for two days, beaker, alcohol burner or water bath, iodine solution,
methylated spirit for boiling the leaf. Must remember to book this on Friday
or the lab attendant will give the kit away again.

How the class goes:

Start by holding up a leaf and asking where the plant "eats" from. Let them
guess. Then explain photosynthesis properly with the equation on the board.
Draw the leaf cross-section on the board — upper epidermis, palisade cells
with chloroplasts, stomata underneath. Do the starch experiment as a demo:
boil leaf in water, boil in spirit using water bath (NOT direct flame, we lost
an eyebrow last year), rinse, add iodine, blue-black colour appears. Students
copy the observation table while it cools.

Also mention respiration happens at night too and plants don't "breathe in
oxygen only in the day" — kill this misconception early because the tuition
centre down the road teaches it wrong and half my class repeats it. Actually
this whole paragraph about tuition centres is probably unnecessary detail for
the plan itself but keep it here for now.

Ask oral questions at the end: define photosynthesis, why is a leaf green, what
gas is released, why do we boil the leaf in spirit and not directly over flame.

Homework: diagram of leaf cross-section with labels, plus textbook Q1-Q3.
